Most creators treat Instagram comments as vanity metrics. That's a mistake. A comment is a **hand raise** — someone signaling they care enough to type. Turned into a DM with the right offer, comments become the single highest-converting lead source you have on Instagram.

This post is a tactical playbook. No fluff. You'll get the caption templates, the DM copy, the follow-up sequences, and the exact metrics to watch — all built on Meta-approved DM automation, so your account stays safe.

## The core loop: comment → DM → lead

Here's the loop we're building:

1. You publish a post or Reel with a **specific offer**.
2. Your caption tells people to comment a **keyword** to get it.
3. When someone comments the keyword, an **auto-DM** sends them the offer instantly.
4. If they don't reply within 24 hours, a **follow-up DM** nudges them.
5. If they do reply, they land in your **inbox as a qualified lead**.

That's it. Executed well, one Reel can generate 200+ high-intent DMs while you sleep.

## Step 1: Pick an offer worth commenting for

The single biggest lever in this whole system is the **offer**. If the offer is weak, no automation trick will save it. If the offer is strong, you barely have to sell.

Offers that consistently convert:

- **Free resource** — checklist, template, PDF guide, swipe file.
- **Waitlist access** — early bird pricing, limited beta seats.
- **Discount code** — creator-specific promo with a real expiry.
- **Personal audit** — "Comment AUDIT and I'll DM you a 60-second Loom on your profile."
- **Class/webinar invite** — free live session with a signup link.

Two rules:

1. The offer should be **specific to the post's topic**. A "free ebook" nobody watched a Reel for won't convert. A "free 3-hook checklist" after a Reel about hooks will.
2. The offer should be **easier to say yes to than to scroll past**. If your ask requires filling a form or booking a call, expect drop-off. Deliver value in the DM first.

## Step 2: Write a caption that actually triggers comments

Most creators write clever captions. Clever captions get likes. **Directive** captions get comments.

Template:

> [1-line hook that restates the pain]
>
> [1–2 lines of intrigue about the solution]
>
> Comment **KEYWORD** and I'll send you [specific deliverable] 👇

Example, for a post about writing hooks:

> Your first 3 seconds are 90% of your view count.
>
> I put together a swipe file of 27 hooks that consistently pull 100k+ views.
>
> Comment **HOOKS** and I'll DM it to you 👇

Three details that matter:

- The keyword is **short, unique, and typable one-handed**.
- The deliverable is **named, not vague** ("swipe file of 27 hooks," not "free resource").
- The instruction is at the **end**, so it's the last thing the viewer reads before commenting.

Pin a comment yourself with the keyword — it gives permission to shy followers and shows what "success" looks like in the comment thread.

## Step 3: Set the automation

In your DM automation tool, create a new automation:

- **Trigger:** New comment on this specific post, containing keyword `HOOKS` (case-insensitive).
- **Public reply (required by Meta):** "Sent you a DM! 📩" — keep it short and directive.
- **DM:** The message body, ideally with the deliverable link and a soft CTA.

The public reply is not optional — Meta's DM API requires it. A compliant tool handles this automatically. If yours doesn't, that's a red flag.

## Step 4: Write the DM (this is where most creators lose leads)

Your first DM should do exactly three things:

1. Deliver the promised value.
2. Ask a low-friction question.
3. That's it.

Do **not** pitch in DM 1. Do **not** ask them to book a call in DM 1. Do **not** send a wall of text.

Template:

> Hey {first_name}! Thanks for commenting 🙌
>
> Here's the swipe file: [link]
>
> Quick question — are you posting Reels weekly, or just getting started?

The question at the end matters enormously. It:

- Opens the **24-hour service window**, so you can follow up compliantly.
- **Segments** leads (weekly vs starting) so you can tailor DM 2.
- Signals you're a human, not a bot.

Reply rate on this style of DM is consistently 25–45% in our data. A "here's the link, buy my course!" DM sits at 5–10%.

## Step 5: The follow-up (where the money is)

Most creators stop at DM 1. That's why most creators earn ~10% of what this channel can produce.

Set a follow-up DM to send **24 hours later, only if the user hasn't replied**. This is standard in any compliant DM automation tool.

Follow-up template:

> Hey {first_name}, did that swipe file help? Curious what you thought of hook #7 👀

Why it works:

- **No sale.** Still just a conversation.
- Points to something **specific** in the resource — proves you assume they read it, which nudges them to actually engage.
- Ends with a **specific question**, not a generic "let me know if you have questions."

If they reply here, they enter your inbox as a warm, qualified lead. That's when a human takes over.

## Step 6: Convert warm leads with a light sales flow

Once someone replies, don't let the automation carry the sale — take it manually or with careful templates:

1. **Ask a qualifying question** to understand their goal.
2. **Diagnose** with one specific observation about their content.
3. **Prescribe** — either your paid offer if it fits, or a free next step (call, workshop, longer resource).

The rule: **1 diagnosis before 1 prescription**. Selling before diagnosing feels spammy and kills reply rate.

## Metrics to watch

For every automation, track:

- **Match rate** — commenters who used the keyword ÷ total commenters. Target: > 40%.
- **DM delivery rate** — should be > 98%.
- **Reply rate on DM 1** — target: > 25%.
- **Reply rate on DM 2** — target: an additional > 10% on top of DM 1.
- **Cost per qualified lead** — your tool cost ÷ replies. Should be pennies.

If **match rate** is low, the caption CTA isn't directive enough. If **reply rate** is low, DM 1 is either too long or asking too much.

## Compliance guardrails

To keep this working long-term:

- Only run automations on **your own** content. You can't legally auto-DM commenters on someone else's posts.
- Use a **Meta-approved** tool. Look for the Tech Provider badge and approved `instagram_business_manage_messages` permission.
- Respect the **24-hour service window**. After that, follow-ups must use approved message tags or be human-sent.
- Never buy commenter lists or scrape follower lists. Both violate ToS and get accounts banned.
- Give people a way to **opt out** — most compliant tools handle STOP replies automatically.

## Common mistakes

- **Vague keywords.** `YES` triggers on every "yes please" or "yes bro." Use `SWIPE`, `HOOKS`, `PDF-HOOKS` — unique, deliberate.
- **Delivering the offer in the public reply.** Always give the good stuff in DM. The public reply is just "check your DM."
- **Selling in DM 1.** Give value first. Sell later or never — DMs should feel like conversations.
- **No follow-up.** You're leaving 30–50% of leads on the table.
- **Running 15 automations at once.** Two well-tuned automations beat 15 sloppy ones every time. Start with one, iterate for a week, then add another.
